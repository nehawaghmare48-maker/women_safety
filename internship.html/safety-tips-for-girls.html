Everyday confidence

Safety tips you can use in real life

Choose what feels useful for your situation. You know your surroundings best, and you deserve support without blame or judgement.

👀

Notice what feels right

Pay attention to your surroundings and your instincts. If a situation feels uncomfortable, it is okay to move away or find someone you trust.

🗣️

Use a clear boundary

You can say “No,” “Stop,” or “I’m leaving.” You do not owe anyone a long explanation. If speaking feels unsafe, focus on getting to help.

🤝

Choose trusted people

Identify a few adults or friends you can contact—such as a family member, teacher, counsellor, coach, or neighbour.

📍

Know your safe places

When you are out, notice staffed public places, security desks, shops, or trusted adults you could approach if you need help.

🧭

Make a simple plan

Before an outing, agree how you will get there and back, who you can call, and what to do if plans change.

📞

Keep key contacts handy

Save trusted contacts and local emergency numbers. If your phone is unavailable, ask a safe adult or an official nearby for help.

School, work & getting around

Feel more prepared when you are out.

These ideas may help with planning and support. They do not make you responsible for another person’s behaviour—harassment or abuse is never your fault.

Follow local travel guidance and adapt these tips to your age, needs, and circumstances.

✓
Share your plan with someone you trust.Let them know where you are going and how you expect to get home, if it is safe to do so.
✓
Plan a backup way to get help.Know who you can call or where you can go if a ride, route, or plan changes.
✓
Stay connected in a way that works for you.Keep your phone accessible when possible; agree on a check-in with a trusted person.
✓
Ask staff or an official for help.If you feel unsafe in transit or at an event, move toward a staffed public area and explain what you need.
✓
Leave with support if you want to.You can ask a friend, teacher, colleague, or security staff to accompany you.
✓
Do not worry about being “polite.”You may end a conversation, change seats, or leave when you feel uncomfortable.
Your space. Your choice.

Boundaries and consent matter.

You have the right to decide what feels comfortable for you. Respectful people listen when you say no or change your mind.

💬
You can say no.
You do not need a reason that someone else accepts.
🔄
You can change your mind.
A yes to one thing is not a yes to everything.
🧡
You deserve respect.
Pressure, threats, and unwanted touching are not okay.
🆘
Get help when you need it.
Tell a trusted person or contact a support service.
Your online space

Keep control of your digital life.

You deserve privacy and respect online too. If someone pressures, threatens, impersonates, or harasses you, you can seek help. You do not have to manage it alone.

What feels safest can depend on your situation. If changing a setting or blocking someone might increase risk, talk to a trusted adult or support professional first.

🔐Protect account accessUse strong, different passwords and extra sign-in protection where available.
🕵️Review privacy settingsThink about who can see your profile, posts, and location.
📷Pause before sharingYou can refuse requests for private photos or personal information.
🧾Keep evidence if safeSave messages, usernames, dates, or screenshots before reporting or blocking.
🚫Use report toolsPlatforms may let you report harassment, threats, or unwanted content.
🤝Tell someone you trustAsk a trusted adult or support service to help you decide what to do next.
When something feels wrong

You deserve to be heard and helped.

If someone makes you feel unsafe, try to get near a trusted person or staffed public place, if you can. Tell someone what happened in your own words. You can ask for help even if you are unsure whether it “counts” as serious.

If the first person you tell does not listen, try another trusted adult or support service. You deserve care, respect, and practical help.

A simple help-seeking plan

  1. Think of one or two trusted people you could contact.
  2. Choose a short phrase or message that means “Please call me” or “I need help.”
  3. Know a safe place nearby where you could ask an adult or staff member for assistance.
  4. If you are in immediate danger, contact emergency services when you can safely do so.
Support in India

Help is available.

For immediate danger in India, call 112. The Women Helpline 181 offers support and information; a trusted person can call with you if that feels easier.

If you are under 18, consider telling a trusted adult such as a parent or caregiver, teacher, school counsellor, or another responsible adult. If one person cannot help, keep reaching out to another safe adult or service.
